It decoded the cached public A=`1089-134686-0001` and B=`1995-1826-0003` FLAC files with ffmpeg to 16-kHz mono PCM16, then sent **A A B B A A** as one continuous real-time-paced 50-ms append stream, with 0.500 s zeros after every clip. A is 3.275 s and B is 3.090 s. All four A copies have identical decoded PCM SHA-256 `b52828469c97b5867391d346c440d7afa635b42e6040eaabdbe6ddc8d7ad525b`; both B copies have identical SHA-256 `14e3724571e33f4c529e066d4eff753c5295d33be50fe3e6c705f090eb358ae5`. The complete 22.280-s / 712,960-byte submitted PCM has SHA-256 `a498e5872ad0edb2ebe3fe38d811b09a824e43c6080c1ec5b74870008520c650`. Clip reference labels were used only to assemble and analyze input; no labels, speaker count, descriptions, or references were sent to the model.

The single WebSocket session received `session.created` and `session.updated` (model exact, `output_modalities=["text"]`, Korean target, PCM at 16 kHz, `speaker_detection` threshold 0.5); after the complete 22.28-s paced stream, it sent one `session.finish` and received `session.finished`. Five bounded native speech-start/stop pairs, five completed source transcriptions and five completed responses appeared among 120 received events. No server or client errors were observed. Audio streaming took 22.284 s. The events were saved with reception timestamps in `events.jsonl`; `result.json` retains the effective configuration, input interval/hash proof, native starts, transcripts, completion, timing, and raw-event counts. Those five `speaker_id` values are **1, 1, 2, 1, 1**. They are integer fields on `input_audio_buffer.speech_started`, not `item_id`, response counters, or a locally invented reclustering. The returned A reuse after a B-labeled span directly contradicts a strict claim that Qwen always increments its native speaker ID at each speaker change or never recalls a speaker. The merged B/B interval means this session does not test stability across two individually bounded B turns. Two completed A-turn source transcripts were empty, and the merged B transcription included a phrase from A before the B phrase; other A source transcriptions reproduced the opening utterance. Therefore completion and audio timing are observed, but the source text does not support a claim of six cleanly transcribed turns or formal word-error accuracy. There is no obvious wholesale sample-rate/time-scale corruption: speech boundary offsets track the 22.28-s audio, including the return to A at 15.04 s.

## Prior-run audit and interpretation

The saved earlier `qwen/run.py` sends one `session.update` before audio, loops over the complete original 51.73-s fixture as paced 50-ms PCM16 appends, sends one `session.finish` after the stream, and waits for `session.finished`; there is no intentional connection/ID reset between clips. Its saved `session.updated` confirms the exact model, text-only/Korean output, PCM 16 kHz and speaker detection threshold 0.5. The fixture SHA-256 and 1,655,360 sent bytes agree with the earlier `result.json`. Inspecting the earlier native events, the only speaker/identity-like key in events is `speaker_id` on 13 `input_audio_buffer.speech_started` records; their integer sequence is **1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12**. The two 5s on consecutive bounded speech intervals already disprove a pure per-item counter. `build_segments` associates start/stop by `item_id`, reads the speaker only from that native `speaker_id`, and scales audio millisecond offsets to seconds. The comparison evaluator overlaps those source-time intervals with known clip intervals, chooses the uniquely longest supported ID within each clip, and compares six same-reference clip pairs. Its published 0/6 is consistent with the original saved native events and the 12 distinct per-clip dominant labels; it is a fixture/run-specific recurrence result, not a general API semantic claim or DER.

**Conclusion/correction recommendation:** Do not overturn the observed original 0/6 fixture result on the strength of this control. Equally, do not present 0/6 as proof that Qwen's `speaker_id` is merely a change/turn counter or inherently incapable of stable returning-person labels: this exact-repeat control preserves A's raw ID through an intervening B-labeled turn. The plausible remaining explanations include failure to recognize different utterances by the same six people in the longer fixture, context-dependent diarization, and undocumented provider ID stability limits; this one control does not rank those causes or establish a general model accuracy rate. If the maintained comparison/report is revised, append this qualification and distinguish exact-waveform reuse from different-utterance speaker recurrence; no product, prior artifact, or comparison file was changed here.

## Bounded distinct-utterance follow-up

One further session used `probe.py --distinct` with **A1 B1 A2 B2**, each followed by 0.500 s silence: A1=`1089-134686-0001`, B1=`1995-1826-0003`, A2=`1089-134686-0003`, B2=`1995-1826-0004`. This uses two different utterances per known speaker, not four copies of one waveform. Reference labels were not transmitted. The prior control's `events.jsonl` and `result.json` were not overwritten; this session's native records are in `events_distinct.jsonl` and `result_distinct.json`. The exact model and `session.updated` settings again confirmed text-only Korean, PCM 16 kHz, `speaker_detection` threshold 0.5, with no count/description hint. The 14.080-s PCM stream is 450,560 bytes, SHA-256 `d569edbca911b0db8e5b9239efe3fa5057b16e0763fc1d442a37dd6b303f0fa6`. One paced connection streamed all bytes (14.086 s wall audio), sent `session.finish`, received `session.finished`; 106 events included four bounded native speech turns, four completed source transcriptions and four completed responses, with zero errors.

Native start `speaker_id` sequence was **1, 2, 1, 2**, with four distinct `item_id` values. Each source clip's dominant-duration raw ID agrees with its matching speaker's other, different utterance: **2/2 same-person pairs match, and no A/B dominant pair merges** in this two-speaker control. Edge spillover from native speech boundaries is shown explicitly, not mistaken for another independently inferred identity. Four completed source texts correspond to the expected four spoken phrases (`Stuff it into you ...`, `Better go ...`, `hello, bertie ...`, `might learn something useful ...`); unlike the identical-copy run there were no empty completions or obvious phrase bleed. This is a qualitative source-text sanity check, not WER.

**Updated conclusion:** The six-person, 51.73-s original 0/6 result remains valid **for that fixture and session**, but the same endpoint can reuse IDs across different utterances by returning speakers in a 14.08-s two-person run. Thus the original result cannot be explained by an inherent always-incrementing turn counter, nor by an inherent inability to generalize across utterances. It is consistent with a condition-dependent failure of recurrence on the larger fixture. The two sessions differ in speaker count, length, clip order, and intervening content, so this follow-up **does not isolate speaker-count/context load as the cause**, establish a maximum speaker capacity, or establish expected performance in other speech domains. Recommend that the Director qualify the maintained comparison accordingly rather than changing the recorded original scores. No prior artifacts, product/config, or maintained report were edited.
